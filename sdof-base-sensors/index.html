Base excitation, rotating unbalance, and seismic sensor regimes

Vibration isolation, seismic transducers, and rotating-unbalance machines share one complex triangle: $X = Y + Z$, or $H_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathrm{i}r) = 1 + H_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathrm{i}r)$. Sweep $r = \omega / \omega_{\mathrm{n}}$ and $\zeta$ to watch one SDOF oscillator act as a stiff accelerometer ($r \ll 1$), cross the invariant $r = \sqrt{2}$ isolation point, become a soft vibrometer ($r \gg 1$), and flip $180^\circ$ to stabilize a supercritical rotor's center of mass.

Part 1 · Kinematic phasor closure

1. The complex displacement triangle $X = Y + Z$ governs every base-excited regime

When the base oscillates as $y(t) = Y\cos(\omega t)$, the spring and damper feel only the relative stroke $z(t) = x(t) - y(t)$, governed by $m\ddot{z} + c\dot{z} + kz = -m\ddot{y} = m\omega^2 Y\cos(\omega t)$. Normalizing by $Y = 1$, the absolute phasor $X = H_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathrm{i}r)$ closes the triangle $X = 1 + Z$, landing inside the dashed teal unit circle whenever $r > \sqrt{2}$.

Part 2 · Dual frequency-response curves

2. Invariant $r = \sqrt{2}$ transmissibility crossover versus the relative / unbalance curve

Every absolute transmissibility curve $T_{\mathrm{d}}(r,\zeta) = |X|/Y$ passes through the exact fixed point $(\sqrt{2}, 1)$: adding damping suppresses resonance below $\sqrt{2}$ but degrades high-frequency isolation above $\sqrt{2}$. Meanwhile, the relative curve $|Z|/Y$ is algebraically identical to the dimensionless rotating-unbalance response $MX/(m_0 e)$ and peaks slightly above unity at $r_{\mathrm{p}} = 1/\sqrt{1-2\zeta^2}$.

Part 3 · Seismic instrument design

3. One transducer, two regimes: Butterworth damping maximizes $\pm 5\%$ bandwidth

A seismic sensor reads the relative spring deflection $z(t)$. Making the suspension stiff ($r \ll 1$) yields an accelerometer ($z \approx -\ddot{y}/\omega_{\mathrm{n}}^2$); making it soft ($r \gg 1$) yields a vibrometer ($z \approx -y$). Because $|H_{\mathrm{rel}}(r)| \equiv |H_{\mathrm{acc}}(1/r)|$, near-Butterworth damping $\zeta = 0.70$ maximizes the $\pm 5\%$ flat band for both instruments simultaneously ($r \le 0.59$ and $r \ge 1.69$, satisfying $r_{\max}\,r_{\min} = 1$).

Part 4 · Rotating unbalance & supercritical phase flip

4. Above resonance, the housing flips $180^\circ$ so the combined center of mass stands still

A machine of total mass $M$ carrying an eccentric rotor mass $m_0$ at radius $e$ satisfies $M\ddot{x} + c\dot{x} + kx = m_0 e \omega^2\cos(\omega t)$, giving $MX/(m_0 e) = H_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathrm{i}r)$. Because the combined center of mass lies at $x_{\mathrm{CM}}(t) = x(t) + (m_0/M)e\cos(\omega t)$, its normalized phasor satisfies the exact same triangle identity: $MX_{\mathrm{CM}}/(m_0 e) = 1 + H_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathrm{i}r) = H_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathrm{i}r) \to 0$ as $r \to \infty$.
