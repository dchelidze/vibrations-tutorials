Modal decoupling, forced 2-DOF response, and antiresonance

Once we solve $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u} = \omega^2\mathbf{M}\mathbf{u}$, the mass-normalized mode matrix $\mathbf{P} = [\mathbf{u}_1\;\mathbf{u}_2]$ turns a coupled system $\mathbf{M}\ddot{\mathbf{x}} + \mathbf{C}\dot{\mathbf{x}} + \mathbf{K}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{f}(t)$ into two uncoupled 1-DOF modal oscillators whenever $\mathbf{C}$ is proportional. We visualize mass-weighted orthogonality, selective modal forcing, phasor interference behind antiresonance zeros, and the geometric alignment that separates Rayleigh from non-proportional damping.

Part 1 · Mass-weighted inner product & orthogonality

1. Eigenvectors are skewed in physical space but perpendicular in the mass inner product

For distinct natural frequencies $\omega_1 \ne \omega_2$, the symmetric matrices $\mathbf{M}$ and $\mathbf{K}$ enforce $\mathbf{u}_1^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{u}_2 = 0$ and $\mathbf{u}_1^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u}_2 = 0$ — not $\mathbf{u}_1^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{u}_2 = 0$. Geometrically, $\mathbf{u}_1$ and $\mathbf{u}_2$ are conjugate diameters of the kinetic-energy ellipse $\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{x} = 1$: the tangent to the ellipse at $\mathbf{u}_1$ runs parallel to $\mathbf{u}_2$. Rescaling to mass-normalized coordinates $\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{M}^{1/2}\mathbf{x}$ turns that ellipse into the unit circle and snaps $\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2$ to $90^\circ$.

Part 2 · Free response & modal superposition

2. Modal coordinates decouple coupled motion into two independent 1-DOF oscillators

Substituting $\mathbf{x}(t) = \mathbf{P}\mathbf{r}(t) = r_1(t)\mathbf{u}_1 + r_2(t)\mathbf{u}_2$ and premultiplying by $\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}$ yields $\ddot{r}_j + 2\zeta_j\omega_j\dot{r}_j + \omega_j^2 r_j = 0$ for proportional damping. Each modal coordinate obeys its own independent 1-DOF equation with initial conditions extracted by mass-weighted projection $r_j(0) = \mathbf{u}_j^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{x}(0)$.

Part 3 · Generalized modal forces

3. A force vector excites a mode only through its inner product with that mode shape

Under harmonic forcing $\mathbf{f}(t) = \mathbf{f}_0\cos(\omega t)$, transforming to modal coordinates gives $\ddot{r}_j + 2\zeta_j\omega_j\dot{r}_j + \omega_j^2 r_j = Q_j\cos(\omega t)$, where the modal generalized force is the unweighted dot product $Q_j = \mathbf{u}_j^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{f}_0$ (virtual work done by $\mathbf{f}_0$ along $\mathbf{u}_j$). Placing $\mathbf{f}_0$ perpendicular to $\mathbf{u}_j$ silences mode $j$ at every frequency—even right at $\omega = \omega_j$.

Part 4 · Modal superposition in the complex plane

4. Antiresonance arises from destructive phasor interference between consecutive modes

Each entry of the receptance matrix $\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{i}\omega) = (\mathbf{K} - \omega^2\mathbf{M} + \mathrm{i}\omega\mathbf{C})^{-1}$ splits into a sum of modal phasors. Whether an antiresonance notch appears between $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ depends purely on the relative signs of the two modal residues $R_1 = u_{j1}u_{k1}$ and $R_2 = u_{j2}u_{k2}$.

$$H_{jk}(\mathrm{i}\omega) = \underbrace{\frac{u_{j1}u_{k1}}{\omega_1^2 - \omega^2 + \mathrm{i}2\zeta_1\omega_1\omega}}_{\mathbf{h}^{(1)}_{jk}(\omega)} + \underbrace{\frac{u_{j2}u_{k2}}{\omega_2^2 - \omega^2 + \mathrm{i}2\zeta_2\omega_2\omega}}_{\mathbf{h}^{(2)}_{jk}(\omega)}$$

Part 5 · Rayleigh alignment & modal coupling

5. Proportional damping aligns its dissipation ellipse with the modal axes; general damping couples them

Normal modes simultaneously diagonalize $\mathbf{M}$ and $\mathbf{K}$, but they diagonalize $\mathbf{C}$ only when the mass-normalized damping matrix $\mathbf{C}_q = \mathbf{M}^{-1/2}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{M}^{-1/2}$ shares the exact same principal axes as $\tilde{\mathbf{K}}$. Adding a localized physical damper $c_{\mathrm{loc}}$ rotates the Rayleigh dissipation ellipse off the modal axes, creating off-diagonal modal coupling $\tilde{c}_{12} \ne 0$.

Optional advanced note: Caughey–O'Kelly criterion and complex modes

Real undamped normal modes diagonalize $\mathbf{C}$ if and only if the symmetric matrices $\mathbf{C}_q$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{K}}$ commute: $\mathbf{C}_q\tilde{\mathbf{K}} = \tilde{\mathbf{K}}\mathbf{C}_q$, which in physical coordinates reads $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{K}\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{C}$ (Caughey & O'Kelly, 1965). For a 2-DOF system with distinct natural frequencies, any matrix commuting with $\tilde{\mathbf{K}}$ has the exact Rayleigh form $\mathbf{C} = \alpha\mathbf{M} + \beta\mathbf{K}$.

When $\tilde{c}_{12} \ne 0$, the exact free-vibration modes live in the 4-D Foss state space ($\dot{\mathbf{z}} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}$) as complex conjugate eigenvectors: components $x_1(t)$ and $x_2(t)$ oscillate with a nontrivial phase shift rather than passing through equilibrium simultaneously. The diagonal-modal approximation (neglecting $\tilde{c}_{12}$) remains accurate only when $|\tilde{c}_{12}| \ll |\omega_2^2 - \omega_1^2|/\omega_{\text{avg}}$ (well-separated modes and light damping).

Step-by-step 2-DOF modal analysis workflow

  1. 1
    Form symmetric $\mathbf{M}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{C}$. Choose consistent positive coordinates and verify symmetry ($\mathbf{M}^{\mathsf{T}}=\mathbf{M}$, $\mathbf{K}^{\mathsf{T}}=\mathbf{K}$).
  2. 2
    Solve the mass-normalized eigenproblem. Diagonalize $\tilde{\mathbf{K}} = \mathbf{M}^{-1/2}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{M}^{-1/2}$ to get orthonormal $\mathbf{V}$ and mass-normalized physical modes $\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{M}^{-1/2}\mathbf{V}$.
  3. 3
    Check modal damping $\tilde{\mathbf{C}} = \mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{P}$. If off-diagonal $\tilde{c}_{12} = 0$, the modal equations decouple identically; otherwise use direct complex inversion $\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{i}\omega) = (\mathbf{K} - \omega^2\mathbf{M} + \mathrm{i}\omega\mathbf{C})^{-1}$ or state-space integration.
  4. 4
    Project initial conditions and forcing. Use $\mathbf{r}(0) = \mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{x}(0)$ for initial states and $\mathbf{Q}(t) = \mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{f}(t)$ for forces.
  5. 5
    Reconstruct and audit limiting cases. Recover $\mathbf{x}(t) = r_1\mathbf{u}_1 + r_2\mathbf{u}_2$ and verify reciprocity ($H_{12} = H_{21}$), static limit $\mathbf{H}(0) = \mathbf{K}^{-1}$, and driving-point antiresonance $\omega_1 < \omega_{\mathrm{a}} < \omega_2$.
