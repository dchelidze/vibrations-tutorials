Nonlinear Dynamics Laboratory · Research Program

Research

We study nonlinear dynamics and vibrations through theory, computation, and carefully designed experiments. The work falls into four connected themes.

1. Modal analysis 2. Reduced-order modeling 3. Damage & prognosis 4. Absorbers & metamaterials Full publications →

Core Research Areas

Theme 1

Nonlinear normal modes and modal analysis

We identify nonlinear normal modes experimentally and from data. Our methods track how modal frequencies and shapes change with energy, giving backbone curves and frequency-energy dependence that linear modal analysis cannot capture.

Key papers
  • Characteristic value decomposition: A unifying paradigm for data-driven modal analysis. Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing, 2025. doi:10.1016/j.ymssp.2024.111769
  • Continuation of nonlinear normal modes using reduced-order models based on generalized characteristic value decomposition. Nonlinear Dynamics, 2025. doi:10.1007/s11071-024-10239-0
  • Smooth mode decomposition: Theory and its applications in full-field output-only modal analysis. Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing, 2023. doi:10.1016/j.ymssp.2023.110541
  • Subband decomposition based output-only modal analysis. Journal of Vibration and Acoustics, 2023. doi:10.1115/1.4055135
Theme 2

Nonlinear reduced-order modeling

We build reduced-order models that stay dynamically consistent as amplitude grows, using smooth orthogonal decomposition and manifold-based reduction. The aim is models small enough to analyze and accurate enough to predict.

Key papers
  • Persistent model order reduction for complex dynamical systems using smooth orthogonal decomposition. Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing, 2017. doi:10.1016/j.ymssp.2017.04.005
  • A new approach to model reduction of nonlinear control systems using smooth orthogonal decomposition. International Journal of Robust and Nonlinear Control, 2018. doi:10.1002/rnc.4238
  • Robust and dynamically consistent model order reduction for nonlinear dynamic systems. Journal of Dynamic Systems, Measurement, and Control, 2014. doi:10.1115/1.4028470
Theme 3

Signal analysis, damage diagnosis and prognosis

We use multivariate and nonlinear time-series analysis to track slow damage hidden in fast vibration data. Phase space warping and related methods estimate damage states and predict remaining life in structures and materials.

Key papers
  • Ball tree structure-informed phase space warping: a robust algorithm for dynamic degradation tracking under variable speed conditions. Advanced Engineering Informatics, 2026. doi:10.1016/j.aei.2025.104288
  • Geometry-informed phase space warping for reliable fatigue damage monitoring. Structural Health Monitoring, 2024. doi:10.1177/14759217231174894
  • Fatigue life estimation of structures under statistically and spectrally similar variable amplitude loading. Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing, 2021. doi:10.1016/j.ymssp.2021.107856
Theme 4

Vibration absorption, energy transfer and metamaterials

We design passive nonlinear absorbers and energy harvesters that exploit internal resonance and targeted energy transfer. Current work examines geometry-programmed internal resonance and nonlinear mechanical metamaterials.

Key papers
  • Multifaceted vibration absorption of a rotating magnetic nonlinear energy sink. Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing, 2025. doi:10.1016/j.ymssp.2024.112122
  • Irrational nonlinearity enhances the targeted energy transfer in a rotary nonlinear energy sink. Journal of Computational and Nonlinear Dynamics, 2024. doi:10.1115/1.4065193
