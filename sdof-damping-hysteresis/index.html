Damping mechanisms in the force–displacement plane: hysteresis loops and Coulomb stick-slip

Energy lost in one oscillation cycle equals the area $\Delta E_d = \oint f_d\,\mathrm{d}x$ enclosed by the hysteresis loop. We compare viscous damping (ellipse growing with $\omega$), structural/hysteretic damping (frequency-independent ellipse), and Coulomb dry friction (rectangle, $1/X$ equivalent damping, and finite-time stick-slip termination in the phase plane).

Part 1 · Hysteresis geometry

1. Enclosed area in the force–displacement plane measures energy dissipated per cycle

For harmonic motion $x(t) = X\sin(\omega t)$, plotting the damping force $f_d$ against $x$ reveals three distinct geometries: a viscous ellipse ($f_d = c\dot{x}$), a frequency-independent structural ellipse ($f_d = (\eta k/\omega)\dot{x}$), and a Coulomb dry-friction rectangle ($f_d = F_c\operatorname{sgn}\dot{x}$). Adding the linear spring force $kx$ tilts all three loops around the dashed line $f = kx$ without changing their enclosed areas.

Part 2 · Equivalent viscous damping

2. Matching loop area defines $\zeta_{\mathrm{eq}}$, exposing Coulomb's $1/X$ amplitude dependence

Equating any mechanism's energy loss per cycle to a viscous dashpot yields $c_{\mathrm{eq}} = \Delta E_d / (\pi \omega X^2)$. While viscous damping keeps $\zeta_{\mathrm{eq}}$ constant, Coulomb friction gives $\zeta_{\mathrm{eq}} = 2 F_c / (\pi k r X)$—huge damping at tiny amplitudes and vanishing damping authority at large excursions.

Part 3 · Nonlinear dry-friction phase portrait

3. Coulomb free decay traces shifted semicircles and stops dead inside the sticking zone $[-\delta, +\delta]$

In $\ddot{u} + u + \delta\operatorname{sgn}(\dot{u}) = 0$ ($\delta = F_c / k$), the effective equilibrium shifts to $+\delta$ while moving left ($\dot{u} < 0$) and to $-\delta$ while moving right ($\dot{u} > 0$). Each half-cycle trims the amplitude by exactly $2\delta$ ($4\delta$ per full cycle), producing a straight-line envelope that halts permanently in finite time once a turning point lands inside $[-\delta, +\delta]$.

Part 4 · Forced response comparison

4. Pure Coulomb friction cannot bound resonance when $F_0 > \tfrac{4}{\pi} F_c$

Because dry friction dissipates only $4 F_c X$ per cycle while harmonic drive injects up to $\pi F_0 X$ at $r = 1$, whenever $F_c/F_0 < \pi/4 \approx 0.785$ the harmonic-balance FRF $M_{\mathrm{Coul}}(r) = \sqrt{1 - (4F_c/(\pi F_0))^2}/|1 - r^2|$ diverges at resonance—unlike viscous and structural damping, which grow quadratically ($X^2$) and always cap the peak.

Part 6 · Workflow & code

6. Equivalent-damping analysis workflow and verification code

Modeling non-viscous damping (Inman 2.7)

  1. 1
    Inspect the ring-down envelope. Exponential decay $\Rightarrow$ viscous/structural; straight-line envelope stopping at an offset $\Rightarrow$ Coulomb friction.
  2. 2
    Compute cycle energy $\Delta E_d = \oint f_d\,\mathrm{d}x$. Integrate over one assumed harmonic cycle $x = X\sin\omega t$.
  3. 3
    Extract $c_{\mathrm{eq}} = \Delta E_d / (\pi\omega X^2)$. Note whether $c_{\mathrm{eq}}$ depends on $\omega$, $X$, or both.
  4. 4
    Check resonance boundedness. For pure dry friction, verify whether $F_c / F_0 < \pi/4$; if so, include residual viscous/structural damping.
