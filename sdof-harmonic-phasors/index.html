Harmonic forcing, rotating force-polygon geometry, and phase resonance

Under steady harmonic excitation $m\ddot{x} + c\dot{x} + kx = F_0\cos(\omega t)$, equilibrium is a closed four-vector polygon in the complex plane. We show geometrically why phase resonance ($\phi = 90^\circ$ at $r = 1$) cancels stiffness against inertia, how amplitude peaks shift below $r = 1$, why near-resonant undamped motion beats through two drifting phasors, and how half-power bandwidth measures damping directly.

Part 1 · Complex force-balance geometry

1. At phase resonance ($r = 1$), inertia cancels stiffness and damping alone opposes $F_0$

Writing $x(t) = \operatorname{Re}\big(X\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega t - \phi)}\big)$ turns the differential equation into a geometric vector sum: stiffness $kX$ along $0^\circ$, viscous damping $\mathrm{i}c\omega X$ leading by $+90^\circ$, and inertia $-m\omega^2 X$ opposing at $+180^\circ$ must close head-to-tail onto the external drive $F_0$ at $+\phi$ ahead of displacement.

Part 2 · Frequency response & complex receptance

2. Damping shifts the amplitude peak left to $r_{\mathrm{peak}} = \sqrt{1 - 2\zeta^2}$ while phase stays pinned at $r = 1$

Sweeping $r$ traces the Bode magnitude/phase curves on the left and the complex receptance loop $kH(\mathrm{i}\omega) = 1/[(1-r^2) + \mathrm{i}2\zeta r]$ on the right. Notice that every damping curve pivots through $(r, \phi) = (1, 90^\circ)$ on the pure imaginary axis, whereas the farthest point from the origin (amplitude peak) occurs slightly earlier at $r_{\mathrm{peak}} = \sqrt{1 - 2\zeta^2}$ whenever $\zeta < 1/\sqrt{2}$.

Part 3 · Undamped near-resonance geometry ($\zeta = 0$)

3. Beating is two nearly synchronous rotating vectors drifting in and out of phase

Starting an undamped oscillator from rest ($x(0) = \dot{x}(0) = 0$) excites both the forced response at $\omega$ and an equal-amplitude free oscillation at $\omega_{\mathrm{n}}$: $x(\tau)/X_{\mathrm{st}} = (\cos r\tau - \cos\tau)/(1 - r^2)$. As the two rotating phasors drift in and out of phase, the resultant envelope swells and cancels periodically; right at $r = 1$, the beat period stretches to infinity and produces linear secular growth $\tfrac{1}{2}\tau\sin\tau$.

Part 4 · Energy balance & half-power damping identification

4. Average input power peaks strictly at $r = 1$ with exact half-power width $\Delta r = 2\zeta$

In steady state, average power injected by the drive equals average viscous dissipation: $\bar{P}(r) = \tfrac{1}{2} F_0 \omega |X|\sin\phi$. Because velocity leads displacement by $90^\circ$, force and velocity align completely at $\phi = 90^\circ$ ($\sin\phi = 1$, straight positive-slope line on the right). The two half-power frequencies where $\bar{P}(r) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bar{P}_{\max}$ obey $r_{1,2} = \sqrt{1 + \zeta^2} \mp \zeta$, giving the exact identity $\Delta r = r_2 - r_1 \equiv 2\zeta$.

Part 5 · Dynamic regimes

5. Three frequency regimes and the right engineering lever in each

Regime Frequency range Dominant balance Asymptote & phase Effective design lever
Stiffness-controlled $r \ll 1$ $kX \approx F_0$ $M \to 1$, $\phi \to 0^\circ$ Increase stiffness $k$ (damping & mass barely help)
Damping-controlled $r \approx 1$ $c\omega X \approx F_0$ ($kX = m\omega^2 X$) $M(1) = 1/(2\zeta)$, $\phi = 90^\circ$ Add damping $c$ or detune $\omega_{\mathrm{n}}$ away from $\omega$
Mass-controlled $r \gg 1$ $-m\omega^2 X \approx F_0$ $M \sim 1/r^2$, $\phi \to 180^\circ$ Increase mass $m$ (stiffness & damping barely help)
Part 6 · Verification & code

6. Harmonic steady-state workflow and verification code

Steady-state harmonic recipe (Inman 2.1–2.3)

  1. 1
    Normalize to $(\omega_{\mathrm{n}}, \zeta, X_{\mathrm{st}})$. Compute $\omega_{\mathrm{n}} = \sqrt{k/m}$, $\zeta = c/(2\sqrt{km})$, $X_{\mathrm{st}} = F_0/k$, and frequency ratio $r = \omega/\omega_{\mathrm{n}}$.
  2. 2
    Identify the controlling regime first. Check $r \ll 1$ (stiffness), $r \approx 1$ (damping), or $r \gg 1$ (mass) before plugging into formulas.
  3. 3
    Evaluate complex receptance. Use $X = (F_0/k)/[(1 - r^2) + \mathrm{i}2\zeta r]$ to get both amplitude $|X| = M X_{\mathrm{st}}$ and lag $\phi = \operatorname{atan2}(2\zeta r, 1 - r^2)$.
  4. 4
    Separate phase resonance from amplitude peak. Phase crosses $90^\circ$ at $r = 1$; displacement peaks at $r_{\mathrm{peak}} = \sqrt{1 - 2\zeta^2}$ (for $\zeta < 1/\sqrt{2}$).
  5. 5
    Extract experimental $\zeta$ via half-power width. Measure $\Delta\omega = \omega_2 - \omega_1$ at $-3$ dB ($M_{\max}/\sqrt{2}$) and estimate $\zeta \approx \Delta\omega / (2\omega_{\mathrm{n}})$.
