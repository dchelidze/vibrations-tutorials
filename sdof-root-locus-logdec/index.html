Viscous free-response root locus and logarithmic decrement identification

Sweeping the damping ratio $\zeta$ from $0$ to $2$ moves the characteristic poles of $s^2 + 2\zeta\omega_{\mathrm{n}} s + \omega_{\mathrm{n}}^2 = 0$ along an exact semicircle of radius $\omega_{\mathrm{n}}$, collides them at the critical double pole $-\omega_{\mathrm{n}}$, and splits them along the negative real axis. For underdamped ring-downs, connect the pole angle $\cos\theta = \zeta$ directly to logarithmic decrement $\delta$ and multi-cycle semilog peak regression.

Part 1 · Characteristic root geometry

1. Semicircular $s$-plane root locus ($\zeta \le 1$) and real-axis split ($\zeta > 1$)

For $0 \le \zeta \le 1$, the poles lie at $s_{1,2} = -\zeta\omega_{\mathrm{n}} \pm \mathrm{i}\omega_{\mathrm{d}}$ with constant distance $|s_{1,2}| = \omega_{\mathrm{n}}$ from the origin and polar angle $\theta = \arccos(\zeta)$ from the negative real axis. Above $\zeta = 1$, Vieta's product $s_1 s_2 = \omega_{\mathrm{n}}^2$ forces the slow pole toward $0^-$ as the fast pole races to $-\infty$, slowing down settling relative to $\zeta = 1$.

Part 2 · Experimental damping identification

2. Multi-cycle semilog peak regression rejects measurement noise

For $x(0) = x_0, \dot{x}(0) = 0$, consecutive positive peaks occur exactly at $t_k = k T_{\mathrm{d}}$ with $x_k = x_0 e^{-k\delta}$, so $\ln(x_k / x_0) = -k\delta$ is a straight line of slope $-\delta$. Part 3 · Accuracy limits & settling tradeoff

3. When $\delta/(2\pi)$ breaks down, and why overdamping settles slower than critical

Left: $\delta = 2\pi\zeta / \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$ curves sharply upward above $\zeta \approx 0.30$, where the textbook linear approximation $\delta \approx 2\pi\zeta$ crosses $5\%$ error. Right: the dimensionless $2\%$ settling time $\omega_{\mathrm{n}} t_s(\zeta)$ reaches its minimum between $\zeta \approx 0.70$ and $1.0$ before climbing steadily in the overdamped region due to the slow pole $s_1 \approx -\omega_{\mathrm{n}}/(2\zeta)$.
