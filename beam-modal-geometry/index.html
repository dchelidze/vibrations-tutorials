Continuous beams as infinite-DOF modal geometry

Replacing discrete masses with a continuous Euler–Bernoulli beam turns matrix eigenvectors $\mathbf{u}_n$ into continuous eigenfunctions $\phi_n(x)$ and matrix dot products $\mathbf{u}_i^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{u}_j$ into function-space integrals $\int_0^L \phi_i(x)\phi_j(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = 0$. We visualize the transcendental frequency equations, stationary nodal points, cancelling orthogonality area lobes, and quadratic dispersion $\omega_n \propto (\beta_n L)^2$.

Part 1 · Spatial eigenvalue problem & root-finding

1. Boundary conditions quantize a continuous beam into discrete wavenumbers $\beta_n L$

Separating variables $w(x, t) = \phi(x)q(t)$ in the Euler–Bernoulli beam PDE $\rho A w_{tt} + EI w_{xxxx} = 0$ yields the fourth-order spatial eigenproblem $\phi''''(x) = \beta^4 \phi(x)$ with $\omega_n = (\beta_n L)^2\sqrt{EI/(\rho A L^4)}$. Imposing the four end boundary conditions collapses the continuum of candidate $\beta L$ values to a discrete sequence of intersections.

Part 2 · Function-space inner product

2. Mode-shape orthogonality means positive and negative product lobes cancel identically

In 2-DOF systems, orthogonality was the sum $\mathbf{u}_i^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{u}_j = 0$. In a uniform continuous beam, that sum becomes the $L^2$ inner product $\int_0^1 \phi_i(\xi)\phi_j(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi = \delta_{ij}$. For any two distinct modes $i \ne j$, the positive (teal) and negative (crimson) shaded lobes of $\phi_i(\xi)\phi_j(\xi)$ enclose the exact same area.

Part 3 · Modal expansion & spatial superposition

3. Any beam deflection decomposes into uncoupled modal amplitudes $q_n(t)$

Projecting an initial deflection $w_0(\xi)$ onto the orthonormal basis gives $q_n(0) = \int_0^1 w_0(\xi)\phi_n(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi$, and the free response evolves as $w(\xi, t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} q_n(0)\cos(\omega_n t)\phi_n(\xi)$. Notice how placing a localized pluck or bump on an interior nodal point of Mode $n$ eliminates Mode $n$ from the response.

Part 4 · Bending dispersion vs. strings & discrete systems

4. Bending stiffness spreads beam natural frequencies quadratically rather than harmonically

In a taut string or axial rod ($\partial^2 u/\partial t^2 = c^2 \partial^2 u/\partial x^2$), natural frequencies form integer multiples $\omega_n/\omega_1 = n$ (musical harmonics). Because Euler–Bernoulli bending involves a fourth spatial derivative, $\omega_n \propto (\beta_n L)^2$ grows quadratically—producing inharmonic overtones (why a tuning fork or xylophone bar sounds metallic rather than string-like).

Optional advanced note: Where Euler–Bernoulli beam theory breaks down

Euler–Bernoulli theory neglects transverse shear deformation and cross-section rotatory inertia. That approximation is accurate when the modal wavelength $\lambda_{\mathrm{wave}} \approx 2\pi/\beta_n$ exceeds roughly $10\times$ the beam thickness $h$. For short, thick beams or high mode numbers $n$ where $\beta_n h$ is no longer small, Timoshenko beam theory lowers the predicted natural frequencies and caps the wave speed at the finite shear wave speed.

Part 6 · Analysis recipe & code

6. Step-by-step continuous beam modal analysis workflow

Beam modal procedure (Inman 6.5)

  1. 1
    Identify supports and geometric/natural BCs. Clamped: $w = w' = 0$; pinned: $w = w'' = 0$; free: $w'' = w''' = 0$.
  2. 2
    Solve for dimensionless roots $\beta_n L$. Extract roots from the transcendental equation and compute $\omega_n = (\beta_n L)^2\sqrt{EI/(\rho A L^4)}$.
  3. 3
    Normalize mode shapes $\phi_n(\xi)$. Use cancellation-free exponentials for high modes and normalize $\int_0^1 \phi_n^2\,\mathrm{d}\xi = 1$.
  4. 4
    Project initial conditions and distributed loads. Compute $q_n(0) = \int_0^1 w_0\phi_n\,\mathrm{d}\xi$ and $Q_n(t) = \int_0^L f(x,t)\phi_n(x/L)\,\mathrm{d}x$.
  5. 5
    Truncate and audit slenderness. Keep all modes up to $2\times$ the highest excitation frequency and check $\lambda_{\mathrm{wave}} \gg h$.
